# Pattern: Design Sprint

**Category:** Innovation
**Owner:** Innovation Lead + UX Researcher + UI Designer
**Duration:** 5 days
**Last Updated:** [Date]

## When to Use

When a team needs to:
- Validate a new product concept quickly
- Solve a critical design challenge with high uncertainty
- Make a major strategic decision with a prototype rather than debate

## Not For

- Known solutions (just build it)
- Problems requiring deep technical investigation
- Ongoing incremental improvement

## Day-by-Day Structure

### Day 1: Map & Target
- Establish long-term goal ("In 2 years, [vision]")
- Map the challenge (user journey, key actors)
- Expert interviews (15 min each: business, tech, UX)
- Select a target: which one moment on the map will we focus on?

### Day 2: Sketch
- Lightning demos: competitive and analogous inspiration (3 min each)
- Individual sketching (4-part sketch: notes → ideas → crazy 8s → solution sketch)
- Each person presents their solution sketch

### Day 3: Decide
- Museum-style review (silent, everyone reads, uses dot stickers)
- Speed critique (3 min per sketch, note strengths)
- Straw poll + decider vote
- Create storyboard (15-panel narrative of the prototype)

### Day 4: Prototype
- Assign roles: Makers, Stitcher, Writer, Asset Collector, Interviewer
- Build a realistic-looking facade (not code)
- Aim for "Goldilocks" quality — real enough to learn from
- Prepare interview questions

### Day 5: Test
- 5 user interviews (1 hour each)
- Note-taker records: positive quotes, negative quotes, actions, questions
- Debrief: patterns across 5 users
- Decision: Build | Iterate | Kill | Pivot

## Deliverables

- Sprint brief (going in)
- Solution sketches (Day 2)
- Prototype (Day 4)
- Research synthesis and decision (Day 5)
- Sprint retrospective

## Success Criteria

A design sprint is successful if it produces a LEARNING, not necessarily a validated solution. Learning what doesn't work is equally valuable.
